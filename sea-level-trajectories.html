Sea-level forcing

Create a trajectory CSV.

Use a constant annual rate or import projected levels that change over time. Choose the mode in Simulation.

Download CSV example + instructions

The example values are synthetic. Replace them before research use.

Two columns. One trajectory.

year is the calendar year. sea_level_mm is the cumulative level in millimetres relative to one common reference period. Use one scenario, percentile, and location or documented regional summary per file.

At least two distinct years must cover the complete simulation interval, in increasing order. Annual and decadal samples are accepted. For 2025–2100, include 2020 and 2100.

Prepare the file in a spreadsheet.

  1. Obtain regional cumulative projections from an official source listed below. Choose a scenario, percentile, and location or regional summary.
  2. Open the example in Excel or LibreOffice Calc. Remove the optional first comment line beginning with #. Keep the exact headers year and sea_level_mm.
  3. Replace the years and cumulative levels with your selected data. Convert metres × 1000 or centimetres × 10 to millimetres. Keep the same reference period for every row.
  4. Save as CSV UTF-8. Use commas with decimal points, or semicolons with decimal points or commas. Do not use thousands separators or units inside numeric cells.
  5. In Simulation, choose Year-based trajectory (CSV), select the file, and check Preview curve. Keep a note recording the source, region, scenario, percentile, units and reference period.

Source downloads may need reformatting. The application also provides Save CSV example and Format and data sources for offline guidance.

Enter a measured annual trend directly in Constant rate, in mm/year. Do not copy annual rates into sea_level_mm.

What the model does.

The model interpolates years between samples and subtracts the level at the initial raster year. For the example above, the 2025 level is 20 mm and the rise from 2025 to 2030 is 20 mm. The program rejects years outside the file range.

Each run saves the annual changes and increments in sea_level_forcing.csv. The project embeds the imported samples. Document the DEM vertical datum and the initial tidal-influence height separately.
